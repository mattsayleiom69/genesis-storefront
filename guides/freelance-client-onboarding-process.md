# A Simple Client Onboarding Process for Freelancers

Ask a freelancer about their worst project and the story usually starts in the first week: work began on a handshake, nobody agreed how feedback would work, and the final invoice arrived as a surprise. Scope creep, ghosting clients and slow payments are not really end-of-project failures ??? they are onboarding failures that only become visible later.

You do not need software to fix this. You need a short, repeatable process that every client goes through. Here is one that fits in four phases.

## Phase 1: Contract and setup

Before any work starts: a signed agreement covering scope, price, timeline and what happens when things change; the deposit invoiced and paid; logins, credentials and source material collected; the first milestone date agreed. This phase is admin, and it is the one most likely to be skipped when a client is enthusiastic. Do it anyway ??? the friendlier the client, the more you will regret not writing things down.

## Phase 2: Kickoff

A kickoff call with an actual agenda: what success looks like, who gives feedback and how, response-time expectations, your working hours, how many revision rounds are included, which channels to use for what, and what you need from them to keep moving. Follow it with a welcome email, sent within a day of signing, that confirms all of it in writing. The kickoff exists to convert "I assumed???" into "we agreed???".

## Phase 3: Delivery

During the work: check in at meaningful milestones instead of going quiet, set feedback windows with actual dates, and route change requests through the scope process instead of absorbing them one favour at a time. A project with visible progress and agreed checkpoints rarely ends in a surprise ??? clients who can see where the work is do not panic, and clients who do not panic pay on time.

## Phase 4: Handover and close

At the end: deliver the files and access cleanly, state what support covers after delivery, send the final invoice promptly, and ask for a testimonial while the win is fresh. Ending a project deliberately ??? rather than "sending the files and hoping" ??? is what earns the referral and the repeat work.

## Make it the same for every client

The whole point is that the process is identical each time. When every client gets the same welcome email, the same kickoff agenda and the same checklist, you stop forgetting the steps that would have saved you the trouble ??? and clients experience someone who has clearly done this before, because you have.
