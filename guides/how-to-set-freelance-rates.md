# How to Set Freelance Rates Without Undercharging

The most common way freelancers set a rate: find out what someone else charges, knock a bit off to be "competitive", and hope. It feels safe. It is also why so many freelancers are busy and broke ??? the rate was never connected to their own costs, and every competitive discount widens the gap between what they earn and what they need.

There is a better method. It is not market gossip and it is not pricing philosophy. It is arithmetic.

## Start with what you need to earn

Write down the take-home income you actually need for the year. Add the cost of running the business ??? software, hardware, insurance, accountant, training. Add your tax set-aside. That total is what your rate has to generate over a year, before you have priced a single hour of talent.

## Billable hours are not working hours

You might plan to work forty hours a week. You will not sell forty hours a week. Admin, invoicing, marketing, learning, holidays, sick days and the quotes you do not win all eat time. If you plan around two thousand working hours a year, honest sellable hours might be closer to half that ??? run your own numbers rather than borrowing an example. Divide your target by the honest number, not the calendar number. This one correction is the difference between a rate that pays you and a rate that quietly loses you money.

## Know your floor, then add a buffer

Target income divided by billable hours gives your break-even rate: the rate below which working loses money. Do not charge your break-even rate. Add a buffer for slow months, clients who pay late, and equipment that dies ??? the year will be imperfect, and the buffer is what absorbs it. Your real rate sits above your floor with room to spare.

## From rate to quote

When a project arrives, the rate has to become a number the client sees:

- Estimate the hours honestly, then add contingency ??? work nearly always takes longer than the estimate
- Multiply by your rate, and present the total, not the hourly maths, if that suits the work
- Ask for a deposit before work starts; a third of the total is a workable norm
- If you discount, discount deliberately: a smaller scope at the same rate, not the same scope for less money
- Rush jobs cost more, not less ??? add a rush premium instead of absorbing the panic

## Raise the rate when the evidence says so

Rates are not set once. When you are fully booked, when clients accept quotes too quickly, when your skills have grown and your prices have not ??? raise your rate for new clients, and give existing clients notice. An increase you can justify from your own numbers is far easier to send than one you cannot.
