# How Freelancers Get Paid Faster (Without Sounding Desperate)

Waiting on money you have already earned is one of the worst parts of freelancing: the work is done, the client is happy, and payday arrives whenever it feels like it. The instinct is to blame the client ??? but in most cases late payment is a process problem, not a character problem. The freelancers who get paid quickly are not the pushiest; they are the ones who made paying easy and following up boring.

Here is the system, in order.

## Agree the payment terms before you start

Getting paid faster starts before the work does. Before anything is delivered, both sides should already know:

- The payment terms ??? 14 or 30 days are common choices; pick one and state it
- Who actually receives the invoice ??? a named person, not "accounts@"
- Whether they need a purchase order number
- What happens if payment is late ??? late fees only count if they are in your contract or invoice terms

A client who agreed to "30 days, invoice to sarah@company.com, PO 4211" has nothing to be confused about later. Ambiguity is where late payments live.

## Send invoices that are easy to pay

An unpaid invoice is often one that was hard to process. Before you send:

- Invoice the day the work is delivered ??? not "at the end of the month"
- Put the due date on the invoice, not just "net 30" in the footer
- Include your bank details, a payment reference and an invoice number
- Send it as a PDF to the right person, and ask for confirmation

## Ask for a deposit

A deposit ??? a share of the fee paid before work starts ??? filters out clients who never intended to pay, and means a late final payment hurts them too: you are both carrying risk. On longer projects, break the fee into milestones so no single unpaid amount grows too large.

## Follow a fixed chase sequence

The biggest mistake with overdue invoices is improvising the chase. Too soft and you get ignored; too sharp and you damage a relationship over what might be a lost email. A fixed sequence removes the guesswork ??? polite, brief, no apology, no anger, and the invoice re-attached every time:

1. **3???5 days before the due date** ??? a friendly reminder. Many "late" payments are just the client's internal processes being slow; this catches an invoice before it is ever late.
2. **On the due date** ??? a short, matter-of-fact nudge.
3. **7 days overdue** ??? a firm but polite email that asks for a specific payment date, not a vague promise.
4. **14 days overdue** ??? a final notice stating a real deadline and a real consequence ??? and only consequences you will act on, like pausing further work or applying the late fee already in your terms.

Then stop chasing and escalate through the process in your contract, not through angry emails ??? and keep the sequence identical for every client, so how firmly to chase is never a mid-chase decision.
